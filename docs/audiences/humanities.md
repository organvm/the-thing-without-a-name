## A work made from a delay

Danse begins twice. The first beginning is a dancer session photographed on
20 June 2017 and a hand-cut composite made on 25 July. The second is the later
construction of a program capable of returning to that finite record without
turning it into a fixed loop.

The nine-year interval is therefore not empty background. It makes the work an
act of media archaeology: an earlier picture is not merely digitized or
animated, but treated as evidence from which its own compositional grammar can
be measured, reconstructed, and changed.

## The photograph as image and coordinate system

Every source frame records an instant, but the locked camera also records one
stable position in a room. Danse uses both facts. Registered fragments may move
onto different planes and depths while projective texturing keeps architectural
lines continuous from the camera's original viewpoint.

That technical decision has a conceptual consequence. The room is not a
background behind a dancer. It is the common measure that allows separated
moments of a body to remain parts of one place.

## Simultaneity after the picture plane

The long-form project record connects the 2017 composite to analytic cubism's
simultaneity: several views or moments coexist on one surface. Danse does not
make a general art-historical claim that software completes cubism. It asks a
narrower formal question: what changes when fragments that were flattened onto
one picture plane can again occupy depth?

The parameter called `projK` makes that question operational. At one extreme a
plane carries its assigned crop; at the other it becomes a window onto the
shared projected room. The work can move between image-as-object and
image-as-aperture without changing its source material.

## History, identity, and the seeded river

The memory model is small but philosophically consequential. A visit is named
by a seed and an epoch. The seed defines a deterministic river; elapsed time
determines where the visitor encounters it. Returning does not restore the
frame that was last seen. It rejoins a history presumed to have continued.

The system can also cite a moment exactly. This produces a useful tension:

- the work is experienced as passing and unbounded;
- any declared seed/time state is reproducible;
- a film capture is finite but is not equated with the work;
- persistence belongs to a river identity, not to a saved sequence of frames.

Reproducibility here is not the opposite of ephemerality. It is the condition
that lets an ephemeral encounter become discussable and inspectable.

## Authorship and provenance

The repository names Anthony J. Padavano as the artist and binds output to a
tracked photographic corpus. Its declared practice is provenance-first: the
pose model measures; it does not synthesize a body, and deterministic rendering
recombines declared records rather than concealing their origin.

That record does not erase other people. The performer remains deliberately
unnamed in public files while credit and release wording are pending. Music by
Léo Delibes, source arrangements by Paul De Bra, and the vendored MediaPipe
runtime/model have separate attribution and rights layers. The current rights
register is `draft`, so repository-level provenance must not be translated into
final permission for every public, festival, or promotional use.

## Ethical tensions

The same system that makes a source traceable can also intensify its reuse.
Danse therefore treats rights, custody, and publication as distinct from
technical reproducibility. A frame being derivable does not make it cleared; a
press still being generatable does not make it approved; a visitor's camera
being computationally useful does not justify recording or transmission.

The local interaction boundary is one response: camera use is optional,
reduction happens locally, and the work remains complete without embodied input.
The rights register is another: unresolved performer, pictured-object, music,
and promotional questions remain visible as gates.

## Medium and institution

Danse can appear as a living browser work, a finite screendance capture, or a
proposed physical room. Those are not interchangeable containers. Each changes
duration, spectatorship, operational responsibility, and the evidence required
to say the work exists in that form.

At present, the browser artwork has a deployment receipt. The finite package is
still draft, and the room is a reference design with blocked physical gates.
This asymmetry is part of the project's documented state, not an invitation to
describe every possible form as already realized.

## Questions the work opens

- When does a reproducible state become a historical moment rather than a copy?
- What does a camera position remember that an isolated image fragment cannot?
- How does a provenance system change the ethics and aesthetics of recombination?
- Where does the work reside: in the running engine, one visit, one capture, the
  source corpus, or the institutional conditions that present it?
